# Scanned PDFs

[opendataloader-bench](README.md) has no scanned page in it, so it says
nothing about the [OCR tier](../cli/convert.md#ocr) or about the router's
most important signal: a page that is only pixels. This set covers both. It
is synthetic, generated by
[`eval/scanned/make_scans.py`](https://github.com/fluree/fluree-doc-parse/blob/main/eval/scanned/make_scans.py)
with its gold, and scored by `eval/scanned/check.py` against the facts drawn
on each page.

## What is measured

Six business pages (two letters, two account statements with a ruled table,
two invoices with an unruled one) are drawn as born-digital PDFs, rasterised,
degraded and written back with no text layer, under nine scan profiles: clean
grey at 300, 200, 150 and 100 dpi, a skewed feed, a noisy grey scan, a 1-bit
fax at 200 dpi and at fax standard resolution (200 by 100 dpi), and a
quality-35 JPEG. Two more letters are born digital with a screenshot pasted
into them, each written twice, its screenshot captured at 96 and at 192
pixels per inch: four pages, the same text and screenshot on both pages of a
letter. That is 58 pages and 842 facts: names, identifiers, amounts, dates
and phrases. The gold also lists each page's headings, 134 in all: the lines
set apart in bold outside a table (a letterhead, a signature,
`INVOICE`, `Bill to:`, a closing balance), which is what fdoc makes headings
of on the born-digital page; a letter's `Re:` line is a field, its label and
its reference, and read as text. Each fact is said once on its page, so only
reading the place the gold means finds it: the generator draws again any page
that says a fact twice, and the closing balance, which is also the last
row's, is the line `Closing balance: …`.

A fact is found **strictly** when its text is in the text projection
(`fdoc convert -f text`), whitespace runs compared as one space; **as a whole
word** when that occurrence is not inside a longer word or number. Every page
is converted as shipped (no reader, `--no-escalate`) and with the built-in
engine, `--ocr oar-ocr` (PP-OCRv6 small), once each, to DoCO: its nodes'
strings at their character offsets are the text projection.

By kind of fact, with OCR, as a whole word: names 1.000, identifiers 0.940,
amounts 0.996, dates 0.990, phrases 0.973. On the pasted pages, all 52 facts
of the text layer are read with and without OCR, and the 16 facts only the
screenshot holds go from none to all; each letter is two of those pages, so
these are 26 and 8 distinct facts, each counted at both densities.

What this shows:

- **The router finds every scanned page.** Without a reader all 54 image-only
  pages are listed in `doc:unreadPages`, each with the reason `Scanned`, the
  [router's](../design/router.md) verdict for a page that is one large image
  with almost no glyphs. With OCR none is left unread. This is the T3.1 row of
  the test plan, which had no corpus until now. Its only negatives are the
  four pasted pages, two letters each written twice, which hold hundreds of
  characters of text and could not be taken for an unread page: the set has
  no hard negative, such as a born-digital page that is mostly one picture
  with a line of text under it.
- **Born-digital text stays authoritative.** Without a reader each pasted
  page lists its screenshot in `doc:unreadRegions`, with the reason `Small`:
  at 300 by 150 points it is under a tenth of the page, so the router would
  not send it to a model either, and the listing says only that nothing read
  it. With OCR the screenshot is read as a region and the page is never read
  whole, so the screenshot's facts are added and none of the text layer's is
  replaced.
- **The misses are reading errors, not plumbing.** Fourteen facts are
  missed as a whole word. Eight are on the fax at standard resolution,
  characters misread or spaces moved (`Septeunber`, `caiculated`,
  `Transter`, `Brookfield,VT`, `+ 1 555`). Two are 9-point phone numbers
  whose spaces were lost at 200 dpi or in the 150-dpi JPEG
  (`+1555 9011503`), and one a label run into its amount in the JPEG
  (`Closing balance:3,050.56`). Two are an identifier run into the label
  before it, found strictly, not as a whole word: `no.INV-…` on the noisy
  scan and at 100 dpi; and one a phrase on the noisy scan whose space after
  a comma was lost (`payment,Greenway`). Such spaces are not stable: on the
  noisy and skewed 300-dpi pages the detector boxes `Invoice no.` and its
  number as one line, and the same line boxed a pixel or two taller or
  shorter is enough for the recognizer to keep or drop the space after
  `no.`, or one in a phone number. Read at their own resolution rather than
  shrunk to 2,000 px, the 300-dpi pages kept one such space and lost two, and
  the 200-dpi one one fewer.
- **Identifiers are the weak kind**, as in the engine bake-off: a lost space
  or a misread character costs the whole fact.
- **The structure of a scan is not read the way the born-digital page's
  is.** On the four pasted pages fdoc reads the 8 headings of the gold,
  with and without OCR. On the 54 scans, OCR reads 80 headings for 126 in
  the gold, 78 of them the gold's and 2 not; counted page by page, that
  is 46 fewer than the gold and none over (the *missing* and *extra*
  columns), and 46 of the 54 pages have a heading count other than the
  gold's. Most of the gold's headings are bold lines of body size (a
  signature, `Bill to:`, `Closing balance`), and OCR sees no font: the
  engine measures each word's ink (see the [OCR words
  format](../integration/sidecar-formats.md)), its weight from its stems as
  well as its size, so at 200 and 300 dpi most such lines read as
  headings; at 100 and 150 dpi and on faxes a stem is a pixel or two, most
  words are left without a weight, and the line is lost. Measured by its
  ink, a line is sized to within a few percent of its type, where its box
  alone said it to about 5% either way; a line within a tenth of its
  page's body is set at the body's size, and an address or a paragraph is
  read as one block, as on the born-digital page, not line by line. Of the
  2 headings read that are not the gold's, one is a letterhead read with
  two of its words run together in a JPEG (`Harrow OfficeSupply`), and one
  a bank's name and its statement's subtitle read as one line in a JPEG
  (`Silverpine Bank Statement of account`), which loses the gold's
  `Silverpine Bank` there. A signature on a fax at standard resolution
  (`Taylor Example`) is lost too: its stems are a pixel. The
  per-document headings are in the baseline, and a page that no longer
  reads one of its gold's, or reads one other than those it read then,
  fails the check.

*Missing* and *extra* are a page's headings fewer or more than its gold's,
summed; *gold read* counts the gold's headings read as headings, word for
word.

Speed is not recorded here, as it moves with the engine and the machine:
`check.py` prints how long each run takes, and for the OCR run its seconds
per page at the number of workers it was given (`-j`, four by default). The
pages are 0.9 to 8.4 megapixels, each read once.

## Reproducing it

```bash
uv run eval/scanned/make_scans.py          # the set, into eval/scanned/corpus/
cargo build --release
eval/scanned/check.py --models <ocr-assets>
```

The assets directory is the one
[`tools/ocr-assets/build.py`](../cli/ocr.md#the-assets-directory) builds.
`check.py --no-ocr` runs the routing half without it. The generator's
dependency versions are pinned, its interpreter is one uv manages, and its
seeds are fixed, so the set is byte-identical from run to run on one machine;
`check.py` prints its digest. On another platform the bytes can differ (the
interpreter's zlib, the pdfium and Pillow wheels built for it), and with them
a few readings: the baseline's digest then does not match, and `--baseline`
fails, as a fact lost may then be the pixels rather than fdoc;
`--allow-digest-mismatch` compares all the same, with a warning.
`eval/scanned/baseline.json` records the facts each page misses today and
the headings it reads, and `--baseline` fails on any fact lost against it,
on any heading of the gold a page no longer reads as one, on any heading
other than the gold's a page reads that it did not, on a page it does not
list or one it lists that the set does not hold, and on a baseline that lacks
one of its lists.

None of this runs in CI: the engine needs ONNX Runtime and the models, about
100 MB of assets, and the generator needs Python packages the Rust job does
not install. `eval/run.sh` runs the routing half when the set has been
generated, and the OCR run against the baseline too when `FDOC_OCR_MODELS`
names the assets.

## Caveats that belong with the numbers

- **The set is synthetic, and close to the ceiling.** Standard fonts, black
  text on white, one page each, Latin script: no stamps, handwriting,
  bleed-through, phone photos, colour backgrounds or curved pages. A recall of
  0.98 here says the tier is wired right end to end, not that real scans read
  this well. It is a regression net for the OCR tier and the router, not an
  engine comparison; issue
  [#4](https://github.com/fluree/fluree-doc-parse/issues/4) records a harder
  bake-off between engines.
- **Recall, and the headings.** Text the engine invents is not counted:
  the check credits a fact found and charges nothing for text no page
  holds, so a reading that adds words passes it.
  Of the structure only the headings are: word for word on a pasted page,
  and on a scan by which are the gold's and which are not. A table read as
  text lines scores the same as a table read as a table, and a scan is not
  held to its gold's headings, only to the baseline's: it fails when it no
  longer reads one of the gold's it read then, or reads one other than
  those it read then. The
  *missing* and *extra* columns are counts and do not tell a false heading
  from a true one: most scans read fewer headings than their gold, so a
  false one brings a page's count nearer, and the headings read less *gold
  read* are those that are not the gold's.
- **58 pages.** One fact moves a profile by 0.012 and the whole set by about
  0.001.
